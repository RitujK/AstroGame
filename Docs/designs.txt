UI/UX design system — Cosmic Cadets
Updated: August 12, 2026 (missions 1–4 playable, polar-axis globe drag, night-sky observatory)

---

1. Typography (NASA-inspired)
Font strategy:
Primary heading: Orbitron (Google Fonts) — tech/science feel
Body/UI: Inter — readability
Monospace accents: Courier Prime for data displays (mission IDs, checklist labels)
Font loading: Google Fonts in index.html; Phaser scenes wait on document.fonts before boot
CSS variables: --font-heading, --font-body, --font-mono
Hierarchy: headings use Orbitron; body uses Inter; mission HUD uses Orbitron for titles
In-mission text: Phaser Text uses the same CSS families at 2–3× resolution so canvas type matches DOM (landing / map) crispness. Orbitron HUD labels are weight 700.

2. Color palette
Dark theme (default):
Deep space base: #0a0e27 / #050711
Star/glow accents: #64ffda (teal), #4fc3f7 (cyan), #ffd54f (amber)
Text: #ffffff / #b0bec5 / #78909c
Starfield: subtle nebula washes + sprinkled dots (gold/teal/cool)

Bright theme:
Clean white ground: #ffffff / #fafafa (not peach/earth tones)
Primary CTA: solar orange #ff6b00
Secondary: rose red #e11845
Accent: shiny gold #ffcc00
Galaxy violet #7b2ff7 used in type gradients
Shiny gradient type on landing headings (gold → orange → red → violet)
Star sprinkles on white with soft color accents
Landing hero: cosmos-hero.png (kids + rocket amid Earth, solar system, galaxies, nebulae). Mission Map vignettes: missions/mission-01…10.png (same cast, themed per concept). Mission 1 globe: NASA equirectangular Earth (earth-equirectangular.jpg) on a 3D sphere.

Accessibility:
WCAG contrast on body text
44px minimum tap targets
prefers-reduced-motion disables star twinkle
Mission 4 (night sky) always uses the dark palette even if the app theme is Bright — observatory chrome stays night.

3. App surfaces & layout
Marketing landing (#landing) — public entry:
Hero: brand wordmark, mantra, Launch CTA, theme toggle, cosmos-hero art
Age bands section (Cadets nav scrolls here)
Missions preview: 01–03 → 04–09 bridge → 10 (continuity, not a 1…3…10 bug)
Closing CTA + footer
Nav: Cadets / Missions scroll in-page; no About; Launch enters game

Game shell (diegetic):
Home: cosmic Mission Map trail — zigzag stops with circular vignette art per mission (kids + rocket in that mission’s scene), title/concept/teaser, badge status
Unlock: Mission 1 always open; later stops unlock when the previous mission is complete (locked stops are not tappable)
Profile: Cadet ID card + badges
Settings: theme toggle + accessibility controls (TTS/subtitles/volume UI present; not fully wired)
Onboarding: Cadet Registration (from Launch when no profile)

4. Diegetic HUD
Panel frames, glowing borders, Orbitron labels
Mission Map: illustrated trail stops (not plain numbered orbs)
Mission HUD: objective text only. Pause is deferred (code kept, not shown). Hint is removed from every mission — teaching lives in the visuals, objective line, and lock-in feedback.

5. Theme toggle
Landing header + Settings
Persists as localStorage cosmic-cadets-theme (dark | light)
data-theme on <html>; early FOUC script in index.html
Missions 1–3 follow the app theme. Mission 4 locks night-sky chrome.

6. Mission 1 UX (Earth’s Rotation)
Teaching model (must stay obvious):
- Sun is FIXED on the right; rays point at Earth
- RIGHT half of the globe = DAY; LEFT half = NIGHT
- Vertical terminator is drawn; DAY / NIGHT sit on the halves (no extra “current state” legends)
- Earth is a textured 3D sphere that turns around its north–south polar axis
- Horizontal drag follows the finger: drag right → continents on the front move right (like spinning a real globe)
- Vertical drag does not tip Earth off the polar axis
- Cities (New York, Delhi, Nairobi) stay on real latitudes and ride with the globe
- Side “CITY CHECKLIST” shows Need vs Now
- Progress “Correct: N / 3”
- Fixed starting pose (Asia-facing night), not chaotic random
- Compact: globe centered, checklist stacked; wide: globe + panel side by side

7. Mission 2 UX (Moon Phases)
- Space view: Earth at center, Sun light from the right, Moon dragged around its orbit
- Orbit slider as a second control (left = New, opposite = Full)
- “View from Earth” panel shows the live phase disc + kid-readable cue
- Lock In Phase to score each match (5 phases: New, Waxing Crescent, First Quarter, Full, Waning Crescent)
- Theme-aware chrome; Moon/Earth stay naturalistic
- Compact: panels stack under the orbit; Lock In stays above the slider

8. Mission 3 UX (Seasons & Axial Tilt) — Tilt + Orbit Lab
- Space view: perspective orbit around a fixed Sun; drag Earth along the ellipse (cursor tracking, snaps to Dec / Mar / Jun / Sep)
- Tilt slider 0–45° with a highlighted Earth-like band (~15–32°) and a 23.5° tick
- Axis stays parallel through the year: N leans right so December (Earth right of Sun) = North Pole away from the Sun
- Kid-friendly place cards (New York / Delhi / Sydney) update live: season, sun height, day length
- Four Lock In scenarios (NH winter, NH summer, SH winter, SH summer)
- Desktop: orbit + tilt sliders side by side with labels over each track; compact: stacked sliders
- Same panel language and fonts as missions 1–2

9. Mission 4 UX (Star Patterns) — night-sky observatory
- Always dark: immersive starfield (Stellaris-like), no bright-theme wash
- Six patterns: Orion, Ursa Major, Cassiopeia, Leo (New Delhi · March) and Crux, Scorpius (Cape Town · September)
- Connect-the-dots on projected RA/Dec stars; Undo last line
- After a correct pattern: overlay real constellation atlas art (PNG), reveal named stars — no depth/warp animation
- Art + star spacing share a square frame so desktop and mobile stay proportionate
- Then a “real vs scrambled” card pick; cycle all six then complete

10. Responsive
Tablet-first; landing collapses nav links on narrow widths
Mission 1: checklist + globe side-by-side on desktop, globe centered with panel on phones
Missions 2–3: reflow panels and controls on resize (Phaser RESIZE)
Mission 4: square sky frame scales uniformly

11. Motion
Landing star twinkle (subtle); Mission Map nebula drift + star twinkle; theme color transition
Mission success: short fade-in (no scale-pop that warps type)
Mission 4: no post-complete mesh distortion
Reduce motion respected for map, landing stars, and Mission 4 extras
